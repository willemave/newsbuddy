# Train Newsly to earn your attention

**First audit the `to_read` / `skip` judgment Newsly already produces; then test a small, locally trained relevance model if personal selection still needs improvement.** Compare both against the current feed using your explicit choices, and introduce a reversible **Recommended / All** view only after the result improves your daily reading. A regularized linear classifier over existing News embeddings is a real trainable decision model; its production inference is a small numerical calculation inside Rust, without a GPU or an always-on LLM service. This uses **hosted embeddings with a local decision head**, not a fully local pipeline. It is an inexpensive way to test whether learned preferences improve selection; it cannot recover extraction and summary costs already incurred. If provider cost also needs reducing, evaluate a separate metadata-only admission stage afterward. The immediate priority is discovering what you consider worth reading, then establishing whether existing judgments or a cheap model can recognize it.

This recommendation assumes the main problem is finding worthwhile stories, rather than enforcing a particular topic exclusion. Research checked the local checkout and current primary model documentation on October 16, 2026. It did not inspect deployed configuration, live usage, current candidate volumes, hardware capacity, or actual preference-model performance. No training, paid provider benchmark, production changes, or product implementation was performed.

A read-only independent design review completed with **Claude Opus 5.5 (`claude-opus-5-5`)**. Its material findings were checked against source; the verified classification baseline, discovery, analytics, and UI findings are incorporated below. An incorrect claim that embeddings exist only for admitted Briefing items was rejected.

## Selection happens before and after processing

There is no single global “top 10 newest” ranker in the code. RSS and Reddit normally have ten-item discovery limits; RSS follows feed document order, whereas Reddit requests its newest items. Hacker News takes fifteen upstream top stories. The News API then pages chronologically through accessible items, with a default page size of twenty-five. Briefing has another bounded admission process, normally forty News items balanced across platform/topic, and its own presentation pagination. These are different selection boundaries. Changing a page size would not teach Newsly what matters to you. ([Feed discovery](/Users/willem/Development/news_app/rust/crates/newsly-providers/src/scraping/feed.rs:64), [Reddit](/Users/willem/Development/news_app/rust/crates/newsly-providers/src/scraping.rs:630), [Hacker News](/Users/willem/Development/news_app/rust/crates/newsly-providers/src/scraping.rs:230), [News ordering](/Users/willem/Development/news_app/rust/crates/newsly-db/src/content_read.rs:404), [Briefing admission](/Users/willem/Development/news_app/rust/crates/newsly-db/src/briefing_refresh/preparation.rs:265))

The useful distinction is **which candidates deserve processing** versus **which processed stories deserve attention**. At discovery, Newsly has titles, source/domain, dates, tags, and variable-quality RSS descriptions or bodies. Later it has generated summaries and key points. Its default lens embedding model is hosted `openrouter:qwen/qwen3-embedding-8b`, and the cached representation combines title, summary, and key points. The cache loader accepts ready representative News items. These rich vectors are available too late for a pre-processing cost gate, and they do not establish coverage of every Article or Podcast. ([Discovery metadata](/Users/willem/Development/news_app/rust/crates/newsly-providers/src/scraping/feed.rs:94), [Embedding default](/Users/willem/Development/news_app/rust/crates/newsly-domain/src/lib.rs:38), [Embedding input](/Users/willem/Development/news_app/rust/crates/newsly-db/src/briefing_refresh.rs:129), [Eligible cached sources](/Users/willem/Development/news_app/rust/crates/newsly-db/src/news_lens_embeddings.rs:68))

An overlooked existing signal deserves the cheapest test: the News summarizer already returns `to_read` or `skip`, with `skip` intended for duplicative, empty, promotional, or low-signal items. That classification is persisted under `raw_metadata.summary.classification`, but Briefing’s News visibility query checks ready status without enforcing this judgment. Audit its distribution and your disagreements before treating it as a filter: it is an editorial-quality instruction, not a personal-preference model, and its duplicate judgment is not proof of overlap with your reading history. Measure worthwhile stories it would suppress before changing visibility. ([Summary rubric](/Users/willem/Development/news_app/rust/crates/newsly-providers/src/news_item.rs:30), [Classification serialization](/Users/willem/Development/news_app/rust/crates/newsly-worker/src/news_item/input.rs:452), [Persistence](/Users/willem/Development/news_app/rust/crates/newsly-worker/src/news_item/repository.rs:812), [Briefing visibility](/Users/willem/Development/news_app/rust/crates/newsly-db/src/briefing_refresh/sources.rs:149))

For the first learned model, reuse available vectors. Learn personal value from explicit feedback and combine the score with understandable recency and source-diversity rules. Keep novelty separate: topical similarity, duplicate identity, repeated reporting of one event, and “I already know this” are different judgments. Existing semantic lens assignment organizes topics; it is not a learned estimate of your interest. Existing event grouping is a composition heuristic, not proof that a story adds nothing for you. ([Lens assignment](/Users/willem/Development/news_app/rust/crates/newsly-worker/src/briefing_refresh/semantic_lenses.rs:128), [Event grouping](/Users/willem/Development/news_app/rust/crates/newsly-worker/src/briefing_refresh/planning.rs:231))

## Teach one useful decision before training anything larger

Begin with about **thirty processed News cards** to establish a consistent rubric: “worth my attention today,” “not for me,” “already covered,” and “uncertain or later.” Allow an optional reason such as wrong topic, shallow reporting, weak evidence, repetitive coverage, or timing. Preserve those distinctions instead of mapping everything except approval to a negative class. Use the first thirty to discover disagreements in meaning and measure labeling effort. Then grow to roughly **150–300 processed News labels** across multiple days, sources, topics, and events. These are practical pilot sizes, not evidence that any particular accuracy is attainable. Do not split this small initial dataset between processed-story ranking and raw-candidate admission; those are different tasks.

Newsly has useful analytics, but they cannot substitute for these labels. Its typed interaction contract currently exposes `Opened`, while generic feedback is a product-feedback message. Saves can seed plausible positives, but read and save tables are mutable state rather than complete historical preference logs. Briefing also marks sources read when their segment passes the reading viewport. **Unread does not mean unwanted, and read does not mean approved.** ([Interaction contract](/Users/willem/Development/news_app/rust/crates/newsly-contracts/src/mutations.rs:38), [Feedback storage](/Users/willem/Development/news_app/rust/crates/newsly-db/src/interactions.rs:7), [Read/save state](/Users/willem/Development/news_app/rust/crates/newsly-db/src/content_actions.rs:16), [Briefing read laws](/Users/willem/Development/news_app/docs/laws/briefing.md:27))

There is also an identity mismatch to audit before reusing News opens: the client sends a News ID to an interaction path that validates against `contents`. Historical events could be rejected or associated incorrectly; production incidence is unverified. ([Client event](/Users/willem/Development/news_app/client/newsly/newsly/ViewModels/ContentDetailViewModel.swift:421), [Server identity check](/Users/willem/Development/news_app/rust/crates/newsly-db/src/interactions.rs:66))

Evaluate the same candidate snapshots under chronological ordering, the existing editorial classification, simple recency/diversity rules, and the trained linear model. Reserve a later calendar window before tuning; keep canonical articles and same-event syndications out of both sides of a split. For events crossing the boundary, purge conflicting training examples rather than moving future information backward. If the pilot contains too few days or positive events, collect more. Threshold selection and preprocessing must use training/validation data, leaving the final holdout untouched. ([Grouped validation](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.model_selection.GroupKFold.html), [Threshold tuning](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/classification_threshold.html))

Judge the result at a fixed reading budget: worthwhile stories in the top ten, ranking quality, duplicate-event exposure, source/topic concentration, and examples of worthwhile stories omitted. Also compare two anonymized daily slates side by side, randomizing their order, and ask which day’s reading you would actually choose. A better click predictor is insufficient if it selects ten interchangeable stories. NDCG@10 can summarize graded ranking quality, but the missed-story review and your slate preference should remain decisive. ([NDCG definition](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.metrics.ndcg_score.html))

## A local classifier earns the first benchmark

The first trained model should be a regularized logistic or linear head over frozen cached embeddings. Compare it with a simpler cosine-to-positive-centroid baseline, especially while labels are few relative to embedding dimensions. Train offline, export coefficients, intercept, feature order, preprocessing, and representation versions, then calculate scores inside Rust. This avoids generative decoding and a new model-serving dependency. Scikit-learn supports logistic-loss linear classifiers; Newsly already has an offline evaluation package and cached-vector replay machinery, although its existing relation/category metrics do not measure personal relevance. ([Linear classifier documentation](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.linear_model.SGDClassifier.html), [Newsly evaluation boundary](/Users/willem/Development/news_app/python/evals/README.md:1))

If that baseline repeatedly misses contextual judgments that matter, the next useful challenger is **`Alibaba-NLP/gte-reranker-modernbert-base`**: a 149-million-parameter English reranker with an 8,192-token maximum and Apache-2.0 license. Test your preference rubric against each short story representation. Hugging Face’s Rust-based Text Embeddings Inference documents ModernBERT reranking support. Its retrieval performance does not establish personal relevance, so it must win on your holdout and fit the intended host’s measured latency and memory budget. ([Model card](https://huggingface.co/Alibaba-NLP/gte-reranker-modernbert-base), [Serving support](https://github.com/huggingface/text-embeddings-inference))

**Qwen3.5-2B is an optional later decision-model experiment**, especially if simple models cannot follow your reasons for accepting or rejecting stories. Try short, constrained, non-thinking decisions before fine-tuning. Its official model card supports text input and flags thinking-loop risks; MLX LM supports local Apple Silicon inference and LoRA training. Neither fact proves that our exact training/export/runtime combination works. Malformed outputs must become “insufficient evidence,” never silent rejection. Keep a human-labeled test set even if another model helps propose training labels. ([Qwen3.5-2B](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.5-2B), [MLX LM](https://github.com/ml-explore/mlx-lm), [LoRA guide](https://github.com/ml-explore/mlx-lm/blob/main/mlx_lm/LORA.md))

No hardware purchase is justified yet. Benchmark challengers on available hardware, measuring cold start, memory, throughput, and failure behavior. A development Mac can train a model without becoming the availability dependency for the production app. Rust should continue owning user policy, durable state, and queue publication; Python remains offline training/evaluation. ([Architecture boundaries](/Users/willem/Development/news_app/docs/architecture.md:24))

## Make the first change reversible and measurable

The first implementation slice should export versioned candidate snapshots and collect explicit labels in an offline pilot, then produce a comparison report. Include the existing editorial classification, user, item, representation, label, time, and optional reason; separate labels from existing read state. This does not initially require new API contracts or client screens. Acceptance evidence is a reproducible dataset, stable rubric, leakage-checked split, and a comparison against current ordering on identical eligible candidates. Add durable product preference events only after the pilot establishes a useful signal.

The second slice should serve the exported numerical model in Rust and record shadow rankings. Verify Rust/offline numerical parity on fixed examples, model-version and input-version checks, and user visibility isolation. Log candidate sets, scores, policy versions, and eventual display positions. Cached vectors are not guaranteed for every ready item. Treat missing, stale, malformed, or incompatible scores explicitly: **retain the item and use the existing recency order**, with an observable reason. Do not make synchronous provider inference a dependency of feed requests. Shadow scoring must not hide stories or change read state. Acceptance requires both ranking evidence and reliable behavior when scoring is unavailable.

The third slice should build Recommended alongside an accessible All/deferred collection, initially for News. **All is a required new UX deliverable:** the inspected app shell has Briefing and Knowledge, and no reachable standalone All-News screen was established. An existing list API does not provide user reachability by itself. ([App tabs](/Users/willem/Development/news_app/client/newsly/newsly/ContentView.swift:45))

Rank the eligible candidate pool before pagination, rather than reordering each chronological page. The current cursor uses timestamp and ID; personalized ordering needs a stable per-user ranking snapshot and versioned cursor so model or preference changes mid-scroll cannot duplicate or skip stories. Acceptance should include scrolling across a model update and checking complete, unique coverage. ([Current cursor](/Users/willem/Development/news_app/rust/crates/newsly-db/src/content_read.rs:423))

All must preserve existing authorization and visibility rules; recommendations must not destroy source reachability or mark deferred stories read. Briefing’s laws require admitted eligible sources to remain reachable, so a change to Briefing composition needs an explicit behavioral design and corresponding law update. Introduce a small exploration allocation for unfamiliar or uncertain material, then tune it from evidence. If selection is randomized, log its probability; a deterministic diversity quota does not create unbiased counterfactual evaluation data. ([Visibility rules](/Users/willem/Development/news_app/rust/crates/newsly-db/src/news_visibility.rs:3), [Reachability and backlog laws](/Users/willem/Development/news_app/docs/laws/briefing.md:17), [Exploration logging example](https://vowpalwabbit.org/docs/vowpal_wabbit/python/latest/tutorials/python_Simulating_a_news_personalization_scenario_using_Contextual_Bandits.html))

If Briefing is the intended surface, place preference selection before composition, at its per-user pending admission boundary, rather than reshuffling already composed chronological segments. The forty-item batch is a processing increment: changing which stories enter first does not reduce upstream work or eventual composition work if everything is still drained. Composition savings would require a deliberate daily budget and deferred-item policy, with reachability designed explicitly. ([Pending admission](/Users/willem/Development/news_app/rust/crates/newsly-db/src/briefing_refresh/preparation.rs:265))

## Saving processing cost needs a separate admission experiment

Once ranking works, measure the processing ledger: discovered candidates, known/cache hits, new unique admissions, extraction, summary, embeddings, and Briefing composition. Cached summaries and representative reuse mean candidate count is not paid-call count. The cost-saving boundary sits before scrape finalization queues enrichment and processing. A later Briefing filter cannot undo those costs. ([Processing reuse](/Users/willem/Development/news_app/docs/architecture.md:410), [Scrape fan-out](/Users/willem/Development/news_app/rust/crates/newsly-worker/src/scrape.rs:757), [Downstream queues](/Users/willem/Development/news_app/rust/crates/newsly-worker/src/scrape.rs:895))

For that separate experiment, collect a broader metadata-only candidate pool before the present source cap, while keeping expensive processing bounded. Label “worth processing,” “defer,” or “insufficient information” using only information available at that moment. Training a metadata gate with generated summaries would leak future information. A small local encoder such as MiniLM is a plausible starting point, but a headline cannot establish article quality. Evaluate valuable-candidate recall, every missed valuable story, abstentions, and actual avoided work. Retain a random audit sample of deferred candidates. ([MiniLM model card](https://huggingface.co/sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2))

Two implementation traps matter. The aggregator corpus serves multiple users, so your rejection must not suppress shared ingestion that another subscriber needs. Also, ordinary RSS discovery can stop at the first known URL; recording deferred candidates as ordinary known items could prevent later discovery. Even today, a newest-first burst of fifteen unseen entries can yield ten on the first run, then stop at the now-known first entry on the next run, leaving five uncaught without another recovery path. Candidate identity, admission state, and cursor semantics need separate durable treatment before widening discovery. ([Shared visibility](/Users/willem/Development/news_app/rust/crates/newsly-db/src/news_visibility.rs:3), [Stop-at-known policy](/Users/willem/Development/news_app/rust/crates/newsly-worker/src/scrape.rs:474), [Backfill policy](/Users/willem/Development/news_app/rust/crates/newsly-worker/src/feed_backfill.rs:120))

The economics are straightforward once measured: **net monthly saving = unique candidates × actually avoided fraction × avoidable processing cost − filtering cost − incremental hosting cost**. Avoidable means work no other user or product path still requires. If broader discovery yields better choices at the same processing budget, that is already a useful outcome. The next decision should come from your first labeled slates and measured stage costs—not from committing to a larger model or buying infrastructure before we know which judgments the cheap model cannot learn.
